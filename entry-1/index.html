1. How customers give feedback

Shows: giving feedback asks little or nothing of the customer.

  • CX is inferred from the conversation, so it asks nothing of the customer.
  • CSAT is a 1–5 star rating offered at the end of the same chat.
  • Feature requests can be made in the conversation the customer is already in; Wiz explains how, and a person logs the request.

Coverage beyond surveys

CX scores conversations whether or not the customer responds to a survey, so BrightMove hears from customers who would never fill one out: 3,272 conversations with a CX score versus 438 CSAT ratings, about 7.5 times as many.

3. How the loop closes

Shows: every negative score is reviewed weekly, traced to a root cause, and acted on.

Each week BrightMove's support team reviews every negative CX and CSAT score, plus the tagged account maintenance conversations. Each is traced to a root cause and acted on through a knowledge-base update, a change to Wiz's guidance, or a product request. When a customer was let down, the team follows up directly. Repeated maintenance work from large customers becomes a backlog candidate for self-service.

Example: an undocumented questionnaire name limit (June 2026)

  1. Jun 26, 2026: the feedback. A customer reported that the Create Questionnaire button was greyed out with no explanation. The questionnaire name had passed a character limit that was not documented anywhere. The customer suggested blocking typing at the limit or making the limit longer.
  2. Review. The team noted that users could not tell when they had crossed the limit, and that the limit was not documented.
  3. Same morning: the fix. A character-limit note was added to the help article Creating Interview Questions & Questionnaires. The product change was logged as not planned for now, to be revisited if the issue keeps coming up.
  4. About 90 minutes after the email arrived: the follow-up. A support team member told the customer: In an effort to clear up any confusion with the current functionality, I have added a character limit note to the article: Creating Interview Questions & Questionnaires. With our resources and initiatives in flight, this is not something we plan to move forward on just yet. If we continue to see an uptick in frustration around this specific area of the ATS, we will revisit your request. We appreciate you bringing it to us, as always, as your suggestions have helped us shape the ATS for the better, time and time again.

Not every request can move ahead of planned development work. When a code change isn’t possible today, the team still closes the gap where it can: in the help center content customers and Wiz rely on.

Example: routine account changes becoming a self-service candidate

Some feedback is not a complaint at all, but a pattern in routine maintenance work. A large partner regularly sends subscription-change requests, such as adding or removing licenses, moving go-live dates, terminations and renewals, by email or Intercom. Each one is handled by hand, and the requests arrive without a standard, auditable structure. A support team member logged a self-service backlog candidate that would let authorized admins submit these changes themselves, with a required reason for each change, checks against contract minimums, two-person approval on renewal adjustments (the person who submits a change cannot approve it), a pending status until approval, and a full audit trail of who made the change, when, what changed, why, and who approved it. This item is a backlog candidate and has not shipped. A related support-side capability, updating renewals in the billing system, has been completed.

4. Case: drag-and-drop status moves in BrightFlow

Shows: a long-standing customer request moving from backlog to release, with customers told in-app at deployment.

A partner first asked in January 2025 for a way to move candidates between BrightFlow swimlanes without a separate status-update step. At the time the request was closed because it was judged to need a full redesign of the view. In March 2026 a support team member checked with the partner on whether it was still a priority. In August 2026 a customer raised the same friction as a blocker to wider adoption by her team, and the request was reopened as a top-priority backlog item. The team built status drop targets inside each swimlane, an activity window with default details, and a company preference that skips the window. After repeated QA and staging test rounds, it shipped in late October 2026 (deployment 2026.13.0).

Customers were told through the “What’s New” post sent with the deployment. The team began publishing a What’s New post with every deployment so that customers learn about releases in-app, after earlier releases had left the customers who asked for them without notice. The post for this deployment, “What’s New Deployment 2026.13.0,” is shown in-app to users on the ATS dashboard and sent once to each user. As of Oct 28, 2026 it had been sent to 562 users and opened by 560. Its first item reads: Move candidates with a drag and a drop. In BrightFlow, drop a candidate’s card right into the status you want and the status updates instantly. Companies can also turn on the BrightFlow Bypass Activity Modal preference so moves save on the spot with default activity details. No pop-up needed!

  1. Jan 2025: partner request logged; closed as requiring a redesign.
  2. Mar 27, 2026: a support team member asks the partner whether it is still a priority.
  3. Aug 25, 2026: reopened as a priority backlog item after a customer request.
  4. Oct 13–23, 2026: built, then tested through QA and staging; final staging pass Oct 23.
  5. Late October 2026: released in deployment 2026.13.0; What’s New post set live around Oct 23.
